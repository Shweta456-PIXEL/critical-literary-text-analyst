# Sample Research Questions

> **ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE.** These questions are generated from the invented passage in `sample_input.md`. They show the *form* of research question development (see `references/analysis_workflow.md`, section 8). Any historical or literary-critical claim mentioned here is a prompt for checking, not a finding.

## 1. From observation to question

| Step | Example |
|---|---|
| Observation | The creek is renamed on the surveyor's map, while the pans are numbered (O2, O3). |
| Pattern (code) | renaming as appropriation; commons turned into units |
| Puzzle | The text gives the surveyor no name, but gives the women's name for the creek. Why? |
| Question | How does the passage use naming to stage a conflict between two ways of claiming a landscape? |

## 2. Generated questions

| # | Scale | Question | Why it works | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RQ1 | text-level | How does the passage set the map, the number and the knotted-thread ledger against one another as competing ways of recording the same landscape? | Answerable from the passage; avoids a yes/no answer; ties form (record-keeping devices) to meaning. **Best fit** for the researcher's stated interest in "land, naming and records". | Build a table of every recording device and who controls it. |
| RQ2 | text-level | In what ways does the narration withhold a name from the surveyor while naming Savitri and preserving the women's name for the creek, and what does this asymmetry do to the authority of the written record? | Focuses on a specific narrative choice (O9) that a summary would miss. | Close-read focalisation across both paragraphs; compare with other passages if the full story is available. |
| RQ3 | context-level | How might the representation of salt-pan land as both "no one's" and "everyone's" relate to documented debates about common property and taxation in coastal administration? | Connects the text to context; invites historical sources. *Possible gap, to be checked* in the literature. | Locate scholarly histories of salt administration; verify every historical claim `[unverified]` until checked. |
| RQ4 | corpus-level | Across a corpus of regional short fiction about land surveys, how are non-written record-keeping practices (knots, songs, oral boundaries) represented, and who is credited with knowledge? | Scales the passage's concern to a corpus; supports comparison. | Build a small corpus (20-30 stories); design a codebook for record-keeping forms. |
| RQ5 | method-level | What would a concordance of naming and counting vocabulary (named, called, numbered, entered, measured) in such a corpus reveal, and what would it miss compared with close reading? | Tests a DH method against interpretation; includes a built-in critique. **Ambitious option.** | Pilot on this single passage and one or two others using a concordance tool; document preprocessing choices. |

## 3. Why some weaker questions were rejected

| Draft question | Problem | Revision |
|---|---|---|
| What is the theme of the passage? | Too broad; answerable by summary | RQ1 |
| Is colonialism bad in this text? | Evaluative and obvious; not evidence-bound | RQ2 |
| Why did the author write about salt? | Needs authorial intention and biography, which the passage cannot supply | RQ3 (context-level, with sources) |
| How does topic modelling analyse the passage? | A 160-word passage is too short for topic modelling | RQ5 (corpus-level, concordance) |

## 4. How to choose

1. **Fit your focus:** RQ1 and RQ2 match a text-centred project.
2. **Fit your resources:** RQ3 needs archival or historical reading; RQ4 and RQ5 need a corpus.
3. **Fit your timeline:** for a short assignment, develop RQ1 with RQ2 as a sub-question.
4. **Check the literature first** before claiming novelty for any question.
